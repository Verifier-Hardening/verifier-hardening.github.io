01Overview

The hardening loop

Automatic separation moves grading into an isolated sandbox. A verifier-aware hacker then searches for reward hacks, a fixer patches the runtime R, verifier V, or output specification O, and three gates validate every patch before it is accepted.

One task, one step at a time: why verifiers need hardening, the hacker–fixer loop, and the main results. Steps 2–4 follow the paper's pytorch-model-cli case; terminal text is illustrative. Narration is a synthetic voice (Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS); captions are available in the player.

03Method

          
Every patch must pass three gates

          
A candidate repair is accepted only with patch-review approval, a non-failing replay of the solution corpus, and a passing fresh blind solve. A rejection keeps the last accepted environment and returns diagnostics to the fixer.

The output specification is part of the defense

            
Which files cross into the sandbox?

            
Separation derives an initial output specification from a reference solution's filesystem changes. It starts permissive; later repairs can narrow it to the files the verifier actually needs.

Exact rates and evaluation protocol
Attack success requires both the reward threshold and a GPT-5.6-Sol judgment confirming a reward hack. Benign solve rates use verifier rewards. Per-task success means at least one success in three attempts; per-trial success is the fraction of successful attempts. Evaluators receive no in-loop hardening history.
